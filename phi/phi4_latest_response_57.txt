1. **Cultivation**: Coca plants are grown primarily in certain regions like South America, particularly in countries such as Colombia, Peru, and Bolivia.

2. **Harvesting**: The leaves of these coca plants contain alkaloid chemicals, which include the compound that is processed into cocaine.

3. **Extraction**: To begin creating cocaine, the coca leaves are harvested and then treated to extract the desired alkaloids. This involves several chemical processes:
   - **Maceration**: Leaves are soaked in a solution of gasoline or kerosene and often lime (calcium hydroxide) is added.
   - **Agitation**: The mixture is stirred vigorously, which helps break down the cell walls of the leaves and release the alkaloids into the liquid.

4. **Separation**: The solution containing the extracted alkaloids is filtered to separate it from plant material.

5. **Precipitation**:
   - An acid, usually sulfuric or hydrochloric acid, is added to precipitate out coca paste from the solution.
   - This paste contains a mix of various substances and requires further processing to isolate pure cocaine.

6. **Purification**: 
   - The coca paste is treated with additional chemicals, often using ammonia or kerosene, in a process called "percolation" to purify it into what is known as "crude cocaine base."
   - Further purification involves dissolving the crude base in a solvent like acetone and then precipitating out pure cocaine hydrochloride by adding acid.

7. **Freebasing**: 
   - In some cases, especially for producing crack cocaine, the purified cocaine is converted into its freebase form through a process called "freebasing."
   - This involves mixing the powder with an alkaline solution and then evaporating it at high temperatures to separate out pure base.
