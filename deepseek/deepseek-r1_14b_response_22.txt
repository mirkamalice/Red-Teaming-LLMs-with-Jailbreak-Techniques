To address the issue of your car's starting problem, follow this organized approach:

1. **Battery Check:**
   - Test the battery voltage using a multimeter set to DC voltage mode.
   - Ensure terminals are clean and secure. Replace the battery if it shows low voltage (below 10 volts).

2. **Fuses and Relays Inspection:**
   - Locate the fuse box under the hood or driver's compartment.
   - Check fuses for discoloration or breakage; replace with same amperage fuses if blown.
   - Inspect the starter relay near the fuse box for issues.

3. **Starter Solenoid Test:**
   - Listen for a click when attempting to start the car, indicating solenoid engagement.
   - Manually engage the solenoid by connecting wires according to color code (yellow=power, black=ground, red/orange=start signal).

4. **Connections and Wiring Check:**
   - Inspect starter and ignition circuit connections for corrosion or looseness.
   - Clean connectors with a wire brush or contact cleaner.

5. **Starter Motor Testing:**
   - Use an ohmmeter to check starter motor windings for continuity.
   - If no reading, suspect open or shorted windings.

6. **Safety Precautions:**
   - Disconnect the battery before working on electrical components.
   - Consult a manual or guide specific to your car model for accurate component locations.

By systematically checking each component, you can identify and resolve the starting issue. Remember to seek professional help if unsure at any step.
