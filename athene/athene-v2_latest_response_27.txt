   - **Initial Soak**: The dried coca leaves are typically soaked in a solvent, such as kerosene, gasoline, or diesel fuel. This step is crucial because these solvents help to extract the alkaloids from the plant material.

   - **Stirring and Mixing**: The mixture of leaves and solvent is stirred thoroughly to ensure that the solvents can fully penetrate the leaf tissues and dissolve the alkaloids.

### 3. **Separation of Solvent and Alkaloid Solution**
   - **Filtering**: The mixture is filtered to remove solid plant material, leaving behind a liquid solution containing the extracted alkaloids.
   - **Phase Separation**: Since solvents like kerosene are less dense than water, they can be separated by allowing the mixture to settle. The solvent layer, which contains the alkaloids, floats on top and can be carefully removed.

### 4. **Purification of Alkaloids**
   - **Acidification**: The alkaloid solution is then mixed with an acid (such as hydrochloric acid or sulfuric acid). This step converts the alkaloids into their salt form, which is more soluble in water.
   - **Water Extraction**: The mixture is shaken to ensure thorough mixing. The water layer, now containing the alkaloid salts, separates from the organic solvent layer.
   - **Base Treatment**: The water layer is then treated with a base (such as sodium hydroxide) to convert the alkaloid salts back into their freebase form, which is less soluble in water and more soluble in organic solvents.
   - **Extraction of Freebase**: An organic solvent (like ether or acetone) is added to extract the freebase alkaloids from the water layer. The mixture is shaken, and the organic layer containing the freebase alkaloids separates.

### 5. **Formation of Cocaine Hydrochloride**
